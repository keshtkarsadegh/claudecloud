# Agent federation shifts from weights to artifacts

Between 2024 and late 2026, federated learning (FL) and agentic AI met along four lines: federated training of LLM agents, privacy-preserving multi-agent systems that span organizational silos, LLM agents that run FL pipelines, and protocol and benchmark work. The clearest finding is a split inside the first line. **Weight-space federation** (FedAgent at ICLR 2026, Fed-SE, FedMOA) runs RL or SFT on local agent trajectories and averages policy or LoRA updates, which requires open-weight models. **Artifact-space federation** (FICAL, SYNAPSE and Typed Federated Artifacts, FederatedSkill, FedSkillGit) leaves frozen or API-only LLMs untouched. It exchanges kilobyte-scale text, skill edits or schema-typed objects, and an LLM, rather than FedAvg, does the merging. Artifact-space work is growing fastest in 2026. In October 2026 Qiang Yang and co-authors gave the area a unifying formulation, "Federated Agent Optimization". It says agent capability lives in policy, memory, tools, reward and structured knowledge, not only in weights, and it frames the area as a three-way trade-off between utility, privacy leakage and communication cost ([arXiv 2610.01195](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01195)). The reverse direction is also maturing. Helmsman (ICLR 2026) and FedAgentBench show that agent teams can produce whole FL systems and automate medical-imaging FL, but they still fail on interdependent tasks whose goals are left implicit. The evidence base is thin. **Only three main-venue peer-reviewed papers are confirmed (FedAgent, Helmsman, and the Federated RAG mapping study in Findings of EMNLP 2025)**, and almost everything else is an arXiv preprint, many from the second half of 2026. The most serious open problems are these: **no artifact-exchange method applies a formal privacy mechanism**, LLM aggregators open a new poisoning surface, a negative result suggests some reported "federation gains" come from formatting, and **no paper yet runs FL over MCP or A2A**.

## Two different problems share the label "federated agents"

A PhD-level reading of this literature should start by keeping two notions apart, because the papers themselves do so explicitly. **FL-for-agents** shares parameters or artifacts across clients to *improve* agents over time. It is learning in the classical FL sense: there is an aggregation step, a global or personalized state, and rounds. **Federated multi-agent systems (federated MAS)** have agents collaborate *at inference time* across trust boundaries, with privacy enforced by controlling what each agent discloses rather than by aggregating gradients. The Emory EPEAgent paper defines "Federated MAS" in this second sense and names its core challenges: heterogeneous privacy protocols among agents, multi-party conversation structure, and dynamic conversation networks ([Semantic Scholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Privacy-Enhancing-Paradigms-within-Federated-Shi-Wan/fb4446f32cfda955229d14824ed82581f6288a5e)). A third direction inverts the relationship: LLM agents act as the **control plane of FL**, selecting clients, choosing aggregators, tuning hyperparameters or writing complete FL systems. A fourth, thinner strand covers **communication protocols** (MCP, A2A and proposed successors) and **benchmarks**.

## Weight-space federation brings RL to agent trajectories

The most rigorous evidence that agents can be trained federatedly comes from **FedAgent ("Federated Agent Reinforcement Learning")** by Canyu Chen, Dawn Song and colleagues, accepted at **ICLR 2026** ([ICLR virtual page](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2026/10019292); [proceedings PDF](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2026/file/efcf4c92de26fdd19b64ce553636d422-Paper-Conference.pdf)). Clients run RL on their own environment trajectories and share only parameters. The paper introduces **FedAgentGym**, which spans four types of LLM agents, two scenarios (WebShop and ALFWorld), three decentralized-setting variations and three heterogeneity challenges. The abstract claims performance "comparable to centralized training" and robustness to heterogeneity. Per-benchmark numbers were not retrieved†. The accompanying library `sunblaze-ucb/FedAgent` implements FedAvg with optional FedProx, and federated GRPO and PPO trainers built on **verl** with GiGPO integrated. Its README also claims a Best Paper Award at the AAAI'26 TrustAgent Workshop and an Outstanding Paper Award at the AAAI'26 PerFM Workshop [WS]† ([GitHub](https://github.com/sunblaze-ucb/FedAgent)). A fork lists a related title, "Is Decentralized LLM Agent RL Robust to Heterogeneity?", which may be a retitled or companion version† ([GitHub](https://github.com/avan2139/FedAgent)).

This work fills a hole that the broader FL-for-LLMs literature left open. Separate notes on federated LLM training found **no reliable source on federated GRPO or RLVR for reasoning models** and judged federated online RL to be under-explored. They also record that convergence theory for even federated DPO only appeared in 2026 ([arXiv 2605.20696](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.20696)). FedAgent's federated GRPO/PPO trainers and **FedMOA**, a federated GRPO method for personalized reasoning under client-specific rewards ([arXiv 2602.00453](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.00453)), are therefore among the first concrete federated online-RL systems for LLMs. Neither has a theoretical analysis covering the non-stationary, environment-dependent data distributions that agent RL produces.

**Fed-SE** takes a cheaper, self-evolution route. Each client fine-tunes on filtered, high-return trajectories, and the server aggregates updates in a low-rank subspace to cut communication ([arXiv 2512.08870](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.08870)). Its reported gains conflict across sources: about **18% average task-success gain** over federated baselines across five heterogeneous environments according to HF Papers ([HF Papers](https://huggingface.co/papers/2512.08870)), versus about **10% over FedIT** with Qwen2.5-7B according to the arXiv HTML ([arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.08870))†. The difference probably reflects different baselines or paper versions. The choice of baseline matters. FedIT averages the LoRA A and B matrices separately, and a 2025 line of "exact aggregation" work (FLoRA, FedEx-LoRA, Fed-SB) treats that as a known source of error ([arXiv 2509.26399](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.26399)). An agent-FL paper that beats only FedIT has not yet shown it beats current federated-PEFT practice.

## Artifact-space federation ships kilobytes, not gigabytes

A second family keeps the LLM frozen and federates what the agent *knows how to do*. Its lineage runs through black-box federated prompt tuning (FedBPT at KDD'24 and FedOne at ICML'25, which treat the LLM as an inference API and optimize prompts gradient-free) ([arXiv 2310.03123](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.03123)). The agentic versions replace prompts with tool knowledge, skills and typed records, and replace FedAvg with an **LLM-as-aggregator**.

**FICAL** (CUHK-Shenzhen, Dec 2024) is the earliest example. Clients use a Knowledge Compendium Generation module (DeepSeek-v2) to write natural-language "knowledge compendiums" of tool-use knowledge. The server merges them into a global compendium stored in a vector database, and agents retrieve from it at query time ([arXiv 2412.08054](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.08054); [Moonlight review](https://www.themoonlight.io/en/review/federated-in-context-llm-agent-learning)). A secondary survey reports FICAL's communication as **0.078 MB of tool knowledge versus 26,000 MB** for parameter-exchange baselines†. That is roughly five orders of magnitude, but the figure was not verified against the original paper ([FAO HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.01195)). FICAL is effectively federated RAG over self-generated tool documentation, and it acknowledges that in-context learning can leak user data ([arXiv PDF 2412.08054](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.08054)).

The 2026 successors move in two directions: more structure, and more personalization. **SYNAPSE** targets federated tool routing across organizations running frozen, heterogeneous LLMs without sharing data or weights ([arXiv 2602.00911](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.00911)). **Typed Federated Artifacts for the Agentic Web** (Chakraborty, Trieu and Gupta, ASU) argues that FICAL's free-text compendiums are underspecified. It replaces them with schema-validated objects whose fields each carry a role, a type and a validation rule, so that per-field privacy, conflict resolution and cross-model transfer become well-defined ([arXiv 2609.06815](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.06815)). Judging by its title, SYNAPSE ("Typed Compendium Artifacts") appears to be a precursor or companion of this paper, but the relationship is not confirmed†. The Typed Artifacts paper is also the most important *critical* paper in the area. Every compendium arm improved GPT-4o per-step tool-call accuracy by **at least 6.7 points on τ-bench retail**, but the authors attribute this to *format rather than federated experience*. On a math proxy and StableToolBench, a **TF-IDF classifier beat every LLM routing arm** ([arXiv HTML v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.06815v1)). A third-party review adds that the benchmark cannot measure routing to unlabeled tools ([Pith](https://pith.science/paper/2609.06815)), and the v1 and abstract wording differ on whether per-field privacy is measured or only specified†. The same first and last authors wrote the EMNLP 2025 Federated RAG mapping study, so this skepticism comes from people who know the retrieval baselines well.

**FederatedSkill** (UCSB with Cisco co-authors, June 2026) federates **structured edits to local skill libraries** instead of raw traces. A server-side "evolution agent" merges those edits into a *personalized* skill set for each client rather than a single global average. Across 20 agent task families it reports **up to +44.4% success rate and −37.5% compute** versus self-evolving baselines ([arXiv 2606.03143](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.03143)), with uploads of about **99–281 KB per task family over a full run** according to FAO ([FAO HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.01195v1)). **FedSkillGit** (October 2026) builds on it and replaces full-document updates with versioned commits ([arXiv 2610.21348](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21348)). Version control is a natural fit here: it gives provenance, diffs and rollback, which are also the first tools one would want against a poisoned skill edit.

The two paradigms differ in more than communication volume. The comparison below draws only on the claims above. The privacy and robustness rows are inferences from how each method works, not measurements.

| Dimension | Weight-space (FedAgent, Fed-SE, FedMOA) | Artifact-space (FICAL, Typed Artifacts, FederatedSkill, FedSkillGit) |
|---|---|---|
| Model requirement | Open weights, local GPU training | Frozen or API LLMs; heterogeneous models across clients allowed |
| What is shared | Policy or LoRA deltas | Text, skill edits, typed records |
| Communication | Model-sized (low-rank compression in Fed-SE) | KB scale (99–281 KB per task family for FederatedSkill; 0.078 MB for FICAL†) |
| Aggregator | FedAvg/FedProx, low-rank averaging | LLM summarizer or evolution agent |
| Personalization | Classical PFL techniques; heterogeneous rewards (FedMOA) | Per-client skill sets (FederatedSkill) |
| Interpretability and audit | Low | High: artifacts are human-readable and, in FedSkillGit, versioned |
| Privacy leakage channel | Gradient or update inversion | The artifact text itself, plus in-context leakage |
| Formal privacy applied | None reported in agent papers | None, per FAO |
| Main threat | Model manipulation and poisoning of updates | Poisoning or prompt injection of the LLM aggregator |
| Peer-reviewed evidence | FedAgent (ICLR 2026) | None |

The artifact paradigm fits the way agents are actually deployed in 2026, around closed API models, and its audit trail is a real advantage. However, it rests on the weaker evidence base. The only careful ablation in the area (Typed Artifacts) found that much of the measured gain came from formatting, and in some settings a classical TF-IDF baseline won. Until papers separate "benefit from other clients' experience" from "benefit from structured context", artifact-space gains should be read as upper bounds on the value of federation.

## Cross-silo agent collaboration relies on disclosure control, not gradients

When agents from different organizations must cooperate on one task at inference time, the privacy problem shifts. The question is no longer "can the server invert my update?" but "what should this agent reveal in this conversation?" **EPEAgent** (Emory, March 2025) intervenes at the retrieval and context-construction stages so that each agent receives only the data it needs, which is a minimal-disclosure principle ([Emory PDF](http://www.cs.emory.edu/~jyang71/files/epeagents.pdf)). **Distributed Agent Reasoning Across Independent Systems With Strict Data Locality** goes further. Each organization keeps its own storage and policies, HMAC-based tokens let organizations coordinate without revealing identities, and coordination runs through short natural-language exchanges ([arXiv 2511.16292](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.16292)). **SplitAgent** motivates an enterprise/cloud split architecture by arguing that MCP and A2A **assume full trust and data sharing** between participants ([arXiv 2603.08221](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.08221)).

Memory is the newest battleground. **AIM** labels each memory private or public and never compares private memories across users, which limits cross-user leakage in multi-agent, multi-user systems ([arXiv 2609.12320](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.12320)). **MemPrivacy** addresses personalized memory for edge-cloud agents, though only its title was confirmed† ([arXiv 2605.09530](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.09530)). **Federated Recommender Agents** keep interaction histories and personal memories on the client and share reasoning as natural-language knowledge, with no parameter updates ([arXiv 2610.39289](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39289)). A position piece on memory as distributed state notes that **no benchmark covers private-versus-shared memory classification or memory integrity under concurrent updates** ([ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/415461413_Memory_as_Distributed_State_in_Multi-Agent_LLM_Systems)). A survey on long-term memory security argues that exposing memory across principals *increases* the attack surface and that poisoned memory can propagate between agents ([arXiv 2604.16548](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.16548v1)).

One paper in this cluster *is* classical FL, applied to agent safety. **Privacy-Preserving Topology-Guided Safety for LLM-Based MAS via Federated Graph Learning** treats training MAS safety guards as a graph-FL problem. Operators keep raw episodes local, and cross-domain federation reportedly **matches centralized pooling** ([arXiv 2609.02967](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.02967)). It is a useful template: the *guardrails* of a multi-agent system can be federated even when the agents themselves are not. **PrivAct** is adjacent but not FL. It trains contextual-privacy norms into MAS through multi-agent preference training ([arXiv 2602.13840](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.13840)).

**Federated RAG** is the most mature substrate for cross-silo agents, and it is the only sub-strand with a peer-reviewed synthesis. *Federated RAG: A Systematic Mapping Study* (Chakraborty, Dahal and Gupta, **Findings of EMNLP 2025**) narrowed about 50 candidates to **18 primary studies**. It catalogues TEEs, homomorphic encryption and DP as confidentiality techniques, and lists privacy-preserving retrieval, cross-client heterogeneity and evaluation as open problems ([arXiv 2505.18906](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.18906); [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-emnlp.388.pdf)). Among the systems it covers, **RAGRoute** reduces redundant cross-silo queries, which is essentially the agent's "which silo do I ask?" decision and overlaps conceptually with federated tool routing. **FedE4RAG** trains retriever embeddings federatedly. One set of notes describes it as using homomorphic encryption, and another reports knowledge distillation plus gradient compression with **up to 94.89% communication reduction** ([ResearchGate mirror](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/391246945_Privacy-Preserving_Federated_Embedding_Learning_for_Localized_Retrieval-Augmented_Generation)). **HyFedRAG** is an edge-cloud federated RAG system for heterogeneous medical data ([arXiv 2509.06444](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.06444)). For agent designers, the warning from the RAG literature is direct. The CCS 2025 Interrogation Attack achieves stealthy membership inference against RAG with **about 30 natural-language queries** ([arXiv 2502.00306](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.00306)), so "the data stays in a local index" is not a privacy guarantee for an agent that answers other silos' questions.

## LLM agents can run FL pipelines but not yet reason across them

The reverse direction, agents as the FL control plane, has the strongest peer-reviewed anchor after FedAgent. **Helmsman** (Haoyuan Li et al., TU Eindhoven; **ICLR 2026**) synthesizes complete FL systems in three phases. A human-in-the-loop planning phase comes first, then modular code generation by supervised agent teams, then autonomous evaluation and refinement in a sandboxed FL simulator. Its **AgentFL-Bench** has 16 tasks across five areas: data heterogeneity, communication efficiency, personalization, active learning and continual learning. Generated solutions are reported as "competitive with, and often superior to" hand-crafted baselines ([ML Anthology](https://mlanthology.org/iclr/2026/li2026iclr-helmsman/); [arXiv 2510.14512](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.14512)). Per-task numbers were not retrieved†. The notes give the code location as `github.com/haoyuan-l/Helmsman`.

**FedAgentBench** (Saha, Strong, Mishra, Ouyang and Noble, Oxford) tests automation at clinical scale. Server and hospital agents handle client selection, orchestration, preprocessing, label harmonization and algorithm selection from a **registry of 40 FL algorithms**, across **201 datasets in six imaging modalities**. It evaluates **14 open-source and 10 proprietary LLMs**. GPT-4.1 and DeepSeek-V3 automate many stages, but **complex, interdependent tasks with implicit goals remain hard** ([arXiv 2509.23803](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.23803v1); [Lacuna](https://lacuna.tiptreesystems.com/work/fedagentbench-towards-automating-real-world-federated-medical-image-analysis/wrk_e7284a8bcb4266a6070a84df10c95959)). It was submitted to ICLR 2026, but acceptance is unconfirmed, so it is treated here as a preprint†.

The finer-grained control papers raise a scaling concern. **Agentic Federated Learning: The Future of Distributed Training Orchestration** compares random selection, a raw-LLM selector and a tool-calling agent for client selection. The **raw LLM performed best**, but its token cost grows with client count and **exceeded the agent's at 50 clients**. The authors also warn that agents can misjudge client utility and repeatedly pick ineffective devices ([arXiv 2604.04895](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.04895v1)). **FL-MAESTRO** assigns three specialist LLM agents to separate decision dimensions, has a coordinator merge them into one decision per round, and vets that decision with a **non-LLM feasibility check** ([arXiv 2608.20518](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.20518)). **FedAgent-HPO** used dual agents for personalized hyperparameter optimization and reported **up to +8.8% accuracy under non-IID data**, but it was **withdrawn from ICLR 2026** and has no peer-review standing ([OpenReview](https://openreview.net/forum?id=q78jTz1JDy)). Note also that "agentic FL" does not always mean LLMs. A September 2026 paper with the same headline uses **rule-based** server and client agents ([arXiv 2609.35914](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.35914v1)), and a DDQN RL agent for client selection in federated LLM fine-tuning is the obvious non-LLM baseline ([ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/392240808_Reinforcement_learning_Agent_for_Client_Selection_in_Federated_LLMs)).

Across these papers a common design emerges: **the LLM proposes and a deterministic component verifies** (Helmsman's sandboxed simulator, FL-MAESTRO's feasibility check). The cost-scaling result implies that LLM decisions made every round fit cross-silo FL with tens of institutions, not cross-device FL with thousands of phones, unless client statistics are summarized hierarchically before reaching the LLM. That is an inference, not something tested in the sources.

## No paper yet runs federated learning over MCP or A2A

Protocols appear in this literature as a *diagnosis* and as *proposals*, not as a substrate on which FL has been demonstrated. The notes found **no paper, peer-reviewed or not, that runs FL training or federated agent learning over MCP or A2A**. SplitAgent's critique that both protocols assume full trust ([arXiv 2603.08221](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.08221)) is echoed by a threat analysis observing that MCP, A2A and FL together widen the attack surface, including private-data leakage between agents ([arXiv 2506.23260](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.23260v1)). The proposals respond in different ways. **ACP** proposes a unified protocol for "secure, federated, and autonomous" A2A orchestration with decentralized identity verification, and names the lack of a federated orchestration model as the main gap ([arXiv 2602.15055](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.15055v1)). **Secure Multi-Modal Data Fusion in Federated Digital Health Systems via MCP** uses MCP as the transport for federated data fusion, though nothing beyond its title was verified† ([arXiv 2510.01780](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.01780)). **Privacy in Action** builds a live privacy benchmark that runs under MCP alone and under MCP plus A2A, and claims its privacy checker is protocol-agnostic ([arXiv 2509.17488](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.17488)). Typed Federated Artifacts is framed for the "Agentic Web" and is, in effect, a protocol-layer proposal for exchanging federated agent knowledge as validated objects ([arXiv 2609.06815](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.06815)). Whether the MCP or A2A specifications themselves gained federation or privacy extensions in 2026 was not checked†.

## Benchmarks measure four incompatible things

Seven benchmarks now fall into four families, and they do not share a metric. FedAgentGym measures *agents trained by FL*. AgentFL-Bench and FedAgentBench measure *agents doing FL engineering*. The tool-routing and skill evaluations measure *artifact federation*. Privacy in Action measures *protocol-level leakage*. None reports utility, privacy leakage and communication cost together, which is exactly the three-way objective FAO proposes ([arXiv 2610.01195](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01195)). The mainstream federated-LLM benchmarks, FlowerTune (NeurIPS 2025 Datasets and Benchmarks) and FedLLM-Bench (NeurIPS 2024 Datasets and Benchmarks), cover federated fine-tuning and, in FedLLM-Bench's case, one preference-alignment dataset, but not agent trajectories or tool use ([FlowerTune](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.02961); [FedLLM-Bench](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/file/c8cdab0e890c59255c27977072fdb0f0-Paper-Datasets_and_Benchmarks_Track.pdf)). FedAgent itself builds on verl rather than on any of the FL-LLM frameworks those benchmarks are based on.

## Privacy guarantees trail the new attack surface

Nearly every method above defines privacy as "raw data stays local". The adjacent privacy literature shows why that is not enough. For weight-space agent FL, **DAGER (NeurIPS 2024)** recovers text exactly from LLM gradients, full batches up to size 128 with ROUGE-1/2 above 0.99, by exploiting low-rank attention gradients and discrete token embeddings ([arXiv 2405.15586](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.15586)). There is also a separate line on **gradient inversion in federated RL** ([arXiv 2512.00303](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.00303)). Agent trajectories contain user requests, tool arguments and retrieved records, so this is a serious exposure for FedAgent- and Fed-SE-style training. Model-manipulation attacks on federated LLM fine-tuning ([arXiv 2605.07961](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.07961)) apply directly as well. For artifact-space FL, FAO states that **none of the text- or rule-exchange methods applies a formal privacy mechanism** such as DP ([arXiv HTML 2610.01195](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.01195v1)). FICAL concedes in-context leakage. A global compendium served from a vector database is exactly the kind of RAG store that the 30-query Interrogation Attack targets ([arXiv 2502.00306](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.00306)). For federated MAS, memory poisoning propagates across agents ([arXiv 2604.16548](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.16548v1)).

The defensive toolkit already exists in classical FL. It has simply not been carried over. Google has trained and deployed **more than 20 Gboard language models with DP-FTRL under ρ-zCDP guarantees between 0.2 and 2**, and now requires DP for all new Gboard LM FL tasks ([ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2023.acl-industry.60.pdf)). Its **Confidential Federated Computations** run server-side aggregation inside TEEs with reproducibly built, open-sourced binaries and device-authorized access policies, which makes server-side privacy externally verifiable ([Google Research](https://research.google/pubs/confidential-federated-computations/)). That architecture maps closely onto the central weakness of artifact-space FL: an LLM aggregator that sees every client's skill edits could run as an attested TEE workload. On the domain side, **FedMentor** applies domain-aware DP to heterogeneous federated LLM fine-tuning for mental health ([arXiv 2509.14275](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.14275)), and **FedMARL-LTI** ([PR], *Applied Sciences* 2026) combines federated multi-agent RL with LLM-compatible threat intelligence for cyber defense ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/app16168278)). Both suggest that sensitive-domain deployments will force the issue. The AAAI'26 TrustAgent and PerFM workshops, where FedAgent reportedly won awards, are where this security-and-personalization agenda is being discussed ([GitHub FedAgent](https://github.com/sunblaze-ucb/FedAgent)).

## Research gaps worth a dissertation chapter

The gaps below are of two kinds. Some are stated by the sources themselves, and those are cited. Others follow from the pattern of the evidence and are labeled as inferences. Taken together, they suggest the field has working systems and no shared science of what makes agent federation safe or worthwhile.

Several limits of the underlying evidence also bound this report and should be closed before citing it in a survey. The on-device-agent query was never run. Per-benchmark numbers for FedAgent, per-task Helmsman results, FederatedSkill's task families and EPEAgent's quantitative results were not retrieved. The FICAL communication figure comes from a secondary source. FedAgentBench's venue and the link between the `avan2139/FedAgent` variant and the ICLR paper are unconfirmed. Many October 2026 entries (FAO, FedSkillGit, Federated Recommender Agents) are only days or weeks old and may change substantially in later versions.

## Conclusion

The main conceptual change of 2025–2026 is that **federation is no longer synonymous with averaging weights**. Once agent capability is spread across policy, memory, tools, reward and skills, the unit of exchange becomes a design choice, and the cheapest and most auditable choice, text or skill artifacts merged by an LLM, is also the one with no formal privacy and no robustness theory. That reverses classical FL's position. There, DP-FTRL, secure aggregation and TEEs are mature, and the open question is utility. In artifact-space agent FL, utility is easy to demonstrate, but the evidence for it can be confounded by formatting, while privacy and robustness are almost unstudied. The highest-leverage contributions are therefore not new federated agent methods but **instruments**: a federation-versus-format ablation protocol, a three-objective benchmark built on FAO's formulation, and the transfer of production FL privacy machinery (user-level DP, attested aggregation) to LLM aggregators.

A second implication concerns where agentic FL will actually be used first. Token-cost scaling, the difficulty of implicit-goal orchestration tasks, and the cross-silo focus of FedAgentBench and the federated MAS papers all point to **cross-silo deployments with tens of institutions (hospitals, enterprises, banks)**, not cross-device phone fleets. There, a single verified LLM controller and a typed artifact exchange are affordable, and the protocols (MCP, A2A, ACP) supply the missing identity and transport layer. A researcher entering the field now can make the most difference by building the first FL loop that runs natively over those protocols with a measurable privacy guarantee. As of October 2026, no paper has done this.

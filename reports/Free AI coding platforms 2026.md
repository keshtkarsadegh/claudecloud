# Open-source agents beat big-lab free tiers

As of late October 2026, the closest you can get to Claude Code without paying is an **open-source terminal or IDE agent (OpenCode, Cline, Kilo Code, Aider, Qwen Code, Goose) running free or local models**, not a vendor's free tier. Claude Code itself still has **no free plan**: Anthropic's pricing page marks it unavailable on Claude Free and starts it at Pro, $17–20/month ([Claude pricing](https://claude.com/pricing)). The big-lab alternatives that do cost nothing are now small, metered tiers. OpenAI says Codex is included in ChatGPT Free and Go but publishes no caps. Google **switched off free Gemini CLI on June 18, 2026** and moved individuals to Antigravity, whose free quotas are unpublished. GitHub Copilot Free gives 2,000 completions a month plus a small credit pool. Kiro gives 50 credits a month, and JetBrains gives 3. AI IDEs have settled on the same deal: **unlimited or generous autocomplete plus a small agent quota**. That pattern holds for Windsurf (now Devin Desktop), Antigravity, Trae and Cursor Hobby. **Zed** stands out because its free tier allows unlimited bring-your-own-key and external agents. Across 2026, free usage got smaller almost everywhere. Billing moved from request counts to tokens and credits. Several free options shut down: Roo Code, the Qwen OAuth free tier, free Gemini CLI, new Amazon Q signups and Firebase Studio. So the only sustainable zero-cost route is to own the agent layer (open source) and get the model from wherever is still free: rotating free models, OpenRouter's `:free` pool, Gemini's API free key, or a local model on your own GPU.

**How to read the sourcing.** The research sandbox could fetch only a few vendor pages in full: claude.com/pricing and the GitHub repositories. Most other official pages (Cursor, Zed, Kiro, GitHub Docs, JetBrains, Google, Mistral) were seen only as search-engine snippets. Many figures come solely from third-party pricing aggregators, which often disagree and go stale. Throughout this report, **"[3P]" marks a claim found only in third-party sources that could not be checked against an official vendor page.** Unmarked claims cite an official page or repository, fetched directly or through a search snippet. Pricing in this market changes every few months, so check any number on the vendor page before you rely on it.

### Open-source agents win because you control the model bill

The open-source layer is the only part of this market where the **agent itself costs nothing and has no quota**. OpenCode is MIT-licensed and terminal-native, the closest in feel to Claude Code. It also ships a desktop app and IDE extension. Its "Zen" service offers rotating free models; examples cited are "Big Pickle", Grok Code Fast and GLM 4.7 [3P] ([OpenCode School](https://opencode.school/lessons/models/); [wetheflywheel](https://wetheflywheel.com/en/guides/open-source-ai-coding-agents-2026/)). **Cline** is Apache-2.0 and VS Code-first. It asks for approval step by step, has the largest community, and offers models marked FREE through its own provider after sign-in [3P] ([KDnuggets](https://kdnuggets.com/5-free-ai-coding-agents-you-should-try-in-2026); [fast.io](https://fast.io/resources/kilo-code-vs-cline/)). **Kilo Code** was rebuilt on the OpenCode server in April 2026. It runs in VS Code, JetBrains and a CLI, and reads Roo Code's mode files. That makes it the natural home for Roo users [3P] ([morphllm](https://www.morphllm.com/comparisons/kilo-code-vs-opencode)).

**Qwen Code** is Apache-2.0 and supports "OpenAI, Anthropic, Gemini, and Qwen APIs" plus "any third-party provider or local model (Ollama / vLLM)" ([QwenLM/qwen-code](https://github.com/QwenLM/qwen-code)). Its once-famous free OAuth quota was cut from 1,000 to 100 requests a day and then **ended on April 15, 2026** ([GitHub issue #3203](https://github.com/QwenLM/qwen-code/issues/3203); [inventivehq](https://inventivehq.com/blog/qwen-code-still-free-2026-shutdown)). **Goose** moved from Block to the Linux Foundation's Agentic AI Foundation, with its repo at aaif-goose/goose since April 2026 [3P] ([DevOps Digest](https://devopsdigest.com/linux-foundation-announces-the-formation-of-the-agentic-ai-foundation-aaif); [knightli](https://knightli.com/2026/05/08/goose-open-source-ai-agent-desktop-cli-api/)). **Aider** is still described as the most mature git-native option [3P] ([KDnuggets](https://kdnuggets.com/5-free-ai-coding-agents-you-should-try-in-2026)). **Mistral's Vibe CLI** is open source, and Mistral's own pricing page says its free plan includes "limited access to Vibe … for agentic coding in your terminal" ([Mistral pricing](https://mistral.ai/en/pricing)).

Two cautions apply. First, **Roo Code is dead**: its repository was "archived by the owner on May 15, 2026", and the extension was shut down, with users pointed to Cline and the ZooCode fork ([RooCodeInc/Roo-Code](https://github.com/RooCodeInc/Roo-Code)). Any Roo-based guide from before mid-2026 is outdated. Second, Charm's **Crush** is source-available under FSL-1.1-MIT, not OSI open source [3P] ([vibecodinghub](https://vibecodinghub.org/blog/crush-coding-agent-review)). Also check one claim before using a Claude subscription with third-party agents. A single August 2026 source says Anthropic **stopped letting Claude subscriptions power tools like OpenCode and Cline** [3P, single source] ([agent37](https://www.agent37.com/blog/claude-code-free-alternative)).

### Big-lab agents are free in name but capped in the dark

**OpenAI Codex** is the only first-party Claude Code rival that officially includes free users. OpenAI's Codex pricing page says Codex is included in Free and Go, that local messages and cloud tasks share the plan's allowance, and that weekly limits may apply ([Codex pricing](https://chatgpt.com/codex/pricing/); [OpenAI developers](https://developers.openai.com/codex/pricing)). Beyond that, the details are murky. OpenAI's own help-center snippet contradicts itself on whether Free and Go include Codex ([OpenAI help](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/11369540-using-codex-with-your-chatgpt-plan)). Third-party guides also disagree on access. One says Free users get only a small model in the desktop app, while another says any plan can sign in to the Codex CLI [3P, unresolved] ([eesel](https://www.eesel.ai/blog/openai-codex-free-access-explained); [inventivehq](https://inventivehq.com/blog/codex-cli-pricing-explained)). No caps are published. Users report quotas resetting monthly rather than weekly, and one Free user hit the wall after **about 30 minutes** ([OpenAI community](https://community.openai.com/t/is-a-codex-free-vs-plus-limits-comparison-possible/1382408); [OpenAI community](https://community.openai.com/t/usage-quotas-changed-to-monthly/1382696)). Treat Codex Free as a taster you can check with `/status`, not a daily driver.

**Google** has spent 2026 taking free tools away. Google's own Gemini CLI discussion confirms the CLI **stopped serving free, AI Pro and Ultra users on June 18, 2026**, which ended the 1,000-requests/day free quota that made it 2025's best free agent. Those users moved to the Antigravity CLI announced at I/O on May 19 ([gemini-cli discussion #27274](https://github.com/google-gemini/gemini-cli/discussions/27274); [geminicli.com quota page](https://geminicli.com/docs/resources/quota-and-pricing/)). Gemini CLI reportedly still works with paid API keys, and with a free API key at about 250 requests/day on Flash [3P] ([inventivehq](https://inventivehq.com/blog/gemini-cli-deprecated-antigravity-cli-migration); [tembo](https://www.tembo.io/blog/gemini-cli-pricing)). **Antigravity's free quotas are the biggest missing number in this market.** Third-party reviews describe unlimited Tab and Command requests plus a weekly agent quota that Google keeps cutting. Community reports put the CLI at "low tens of requests per day". One review says **Claude Opus 4.6 leaves the free tier on November 2, 2026** [3P] ([preuve.ai](https://preuve.ai/blog/antigravity-review); [amplifying.ai](https://amplifying.ai/coding-agents/gemini-cli)). Gemini Code Assist for individuals was reported as offering 180,000 completions and 240 chats a month. Whether its IDE extension is still free after June is unconfirmed [3P] ([dev.to](https://dev.to/alanwest/google-made-gemini-code-assist-free-whats-the-catch-4cae)). Jules' status is also unresolved: one source says it was shut off for free users on June 18 [3P, single source] ([codegen.com](https://codegen.com/ai-tools/gemini-cli/)).

**GitHub Copilot Free** still gives **2,000 completions a month** plus an unspecified allowance of AI credits, with auto model selection only. On June 1, 2026, Copilot replaced premium requests with token-metered credits worth $0.01 each ([GitHub Docs billing](https://docs.github.com/en/copilot/concepts/billing-and-usage/individuals/billing)). Secondary sources still cite **50 chat/agent requests a month** for Free [3P] ([costbench](https://costbench.com/software/ai-coding-assistants/github-copilot/free-plan/)). **Kiro** offers a perpetual free tier of **50 credits a month** with no overage, using "open weight models and Claude Sonnet 4.6, with limits". That makes it the only official free route to a current Claude model inside an agentic IDE ([Kiro FAQ](https://kiro.dev/faq); [Kiro billing](https://kiro.dev/docs/billing/)). A 500-credit signup bonus is reported but unverified [3P] ([usagebar](https://usagebar.com/blog/kiro-pricing-and-free-tier)). Kiro's predecessor, Amazon Q Developer, reportedly closed new free signups in May 2026 [3P] ([usagepricing](https://www.usagepricing.com/blueprint/activity/amazon-q-developer-2026-04-30-deprecation)). **JetBrains AI Free** offers unlimited completion and local models, but only **3 credits per 30 days** for cloud features such as the Junie agent, with no top-ups ([JetBrains Junie licensing](https://www.jetbrains.com.cn/help/junie/licensing-and-subscriptions.html)).

### AI IDEs give away autocomplete, not agents

Cursor's Hobby tier is permanent and needs no card, but it lists only "limited" agent and Tab use and pauses at the cap. Community estimates of about 50 agent requests and 2,000 completions a month probably date from 2024–25 [3P] ([JetAdmin](https://www.jetadmin.io/blog/cursor-pricing-explained-plans-credit-system-and-real-costs-in-2026/); [Verdent](https://www.verdent.ai/guides/cursor-free-trial-free-plan)). Cursor's own help page confirms that **new sign-ups for the free student Pro year closed June 25, 2026** ([Cursor help](https://cursor.com/help/account-and-billing/student-discount.md)). Windsurf switched from credits to daily and weekly quotas on March 19, 2026, and was rebranded **Devin Desktop** in June. Its free plan keeps **unlimited Tab and inline edits** plus a light agent quota ([Devin blog](https://devin.ai/blog/windsurf-pricing-plans); [provenbrief](https://provenbrief.com/story/windsurf-pricing-plans-quotas-and-what-changed) [3P]). Trae moved to token billing on February 24, 2026. Its free tier reportedly keeps 5,000 completions a month and limited SOLO agent use [3P] ([IT之家](https://www.ithome.com/0/923/234.htm); [costbench](https://costbench.com/software/ai-coding-assistants/trae/free-plan/)).

**Zed** is the exception that matters for Claude Code fans. Its free Personal plan is "$0 forever" and gives 2,000 accepted edit predictions, plus **unlimited use with your own API keys or external agents**, including Claude Code and Gemini CLI through the Agent Client Protocol ([Zed pricing](https://zed.dev/pricing); [Zed docs](https://zed.dev/docs/account/plans-and-pricing)). That makes Zed a natural free front end for any open-source agent or free model key. Void, the BYOK VS Code fork, has **paused development** [3P] ([codersera](https://codersera.com/blog/void-ide-complete-guide-2026/amp/)). PearAI looks unmaintained [3P] ([openalternative](https://openalternative.co/compare/pearai/vs/void)).

## Free models decide what a free agent can actually do

Because every open-source agent is bring-your-own-key, your real capacity is set by the model source, and those sources shrank in 2026. **OpenRouter's `:free` models** allow 20 requests per minute and **50 requests per day**. Buying $10 of credit once permanently raises that to **1,000 requests per day** [3P] ([Developers Digest](https://www.developersdigest.tech/rate-limits/openrouter); [TrueFoundry](https://www.truefoundry.com/es/blog/openrouter-rate-limits-your-free-tier-depends-on-what-youve-already-paid)). Google AI Studio still gives a **no-card Gemini API key**, but free limits were cut 50–80% in December 2025. They now run at roughly 5–15 requests per minute and 100–1,000 per day depending on model, mostly on the Flash and Flash-Lite lineup. Sources conflict on whether 2.5 Pro is still free [3P] ([agentdeals](https://agentdeals.dev/gemini-api-pricing-changes); [costbench](https://costbench.com/software/llm-api-providers/google-gemini-api/free-plan/)).

Other free sources are disappearing. Cerebras' no-card free tier reportedly closed to new accounts on July 21, 2026 [3P, disputed] ([claudelog](https://claudelog.com/faqs/is-cerebras-free/); [benchlm](https://benchlm.ai/md/free-tier/cerebras.md)). GitHub Models was reportedly retired on July 30, 2026 [3P, single source] ([fast.io](https://fast.io/resources/github-models-rate-limit/)). Groq's free tier still exists, now with per-model daily limits [3P] ([mydealz](https://www.mydealz.de/deals/sammeldeal-kostenlose-ki-api-kapazitaten-fur-entwickler-gemini-groq-openrouter-cloudflare-workers-ai-cerebras-hugging-face-2783697)). Even 1,000 requests a day is modest for agentic work, because one multi-step task fires many model calls. Fifty a day lasts a few tasks at most.

There are two catches. Free cloud tiers are paid for with data: Gemini's unpaid tier and many OpenRouter free endpoints may log or train on your prompts. The cheapest Chinese-hosted models process data under PRC jurisdiction, which some employers forbid. When you do pay, the cheap options are very cheap. **DeepSeek V4 Pro** is reported at about $0.44 input and $0.87 output per million tokens, and Z.ai's **GLM Coding Plan** starts around $18 a month [3P] ([devtk](https://devtk.ai/en/blog/glm-5-3-vs-deepseek-v4-kimi-k3-2026/); [MindStudio](https://www.mindstudio.ai/blog/open-model-coding-plans-glm-kimi-deepseek)).

**Local models are the only option that is truly unlimited and private.** gpt-oss-20b runs in about 16 GB of memory. **Qwen3-Coder-30B-A3B**, a mixture-of-experts model, takes about 19 GB at Q4 and fits a 24 GB card. Frontier open models such as Qwen3-Coder-480B need about 150 GB even heavily quantized, which is beyond consumer hardware [3P] ([morphllm](https://www.morphllm.com/best-ollama-models); [inventivehq](https://inventivehq.com/blog/run-qwen3-coder-locally-ollama)). Devstral Small 2 (24B) is a strong local agent model. Its reported SWE-bench Verified score is 68–72%, against 46.8% for the original Devstral, but its memory needs are reported inconsistently [3P] ([Kilo blog](https://blog.kilo.ai/p/the-best-local-coding-models-for)). Expect local 20–30B models to handle autocomplete, focused edits and short agent loops well. They still trail hosted frontier models on long multi-file refactors.

## Best zero-cost setups for developing your own code

**For a Claude Code-like terminal workflow**, install **OpenCode** (or Aider or Qwen Code). Start on OpenCode Zen's rotating free models. Add an OpenRouter account and a free Gemini API key as fallbacks, so that when one free quota runs out you switch providers rather than stop. This is the closest free match to Claude Code's agent loop. Expect to rotate models and to hit daily caps on heavy days.

**For an IDE workflow**, use VS Code with **Cline** (more conservative, approve each step) or **Kilo Code** (more modes, Roo-compatible), with their free hosted models or the same API keys. Add free autocomplete from **Copilot Free** (2,000 a month) or **Windsurf/Devin Desktop** (unlimited Tab). Alternatively, use **Zed** as the editor: its free tier lets you plug in your own keys and external agents without limit.

**For unlimited and private use**, run **Ollama with Qwen3-Coder-30B-A3B or gpt-oss-20b** on a 16–24 GB GPU and point Cline, Continue, Aider or Qwen Code at the local endpoint. You lose some quality on hard tasks but never hit a quota or send code off your machine.

**To squeeze out big-lab subsidies**, use **Codex** on a ChatGPT Free account, **Antigravity**, **Kiro's 50 credits** (Claude Sonnet 4.6) and **Copilot Free** as occasional extra capacity alongside the open-source setup, not as its foundation. All of them can shrink without notice, and Google has already shrunk its offer once this year.

**If you can spend a little**, the cheapest upgrades are a one-time **$10 OpenRouter top-up** (to 1,000 free-model requests a day) and **DeepSeek V4** pay-as-you-go at cents per task. Zed Pro costs $10 a month, and GitHub Copilot Pro costs $10 a month for 1,500 AI credits ([GitHub plans](https://docs.github.com/en/copilot/get-started/plans)). If you want Claude Code itself, Pro at **$17 a month billed annually** is the entry point ([Claude pricing](https://claude.com/pricing)).

**Students and open-source maintainers** have the best free deals. Copilot Pro is free for verified teachers and maintainers of popular open-source projects ([GitHub plans](https://docs.github.com/en/copilot/get-started/plans)). The Copilot Student plan gives unlimited completions plus 200 credits a month, though students report burning through those credits within days ([GitHub discussion #189268](https://github.com/orgs/community/discussions/189268)). Zed gives verified university students a free year of Pro, launched March 2026 [3P] ([AlternativeTo](https://alternativeto.net/news/2026/3/zed-introduces-free-student-plan-with-pro-features-for-university-students)). Kiro reportedly offers a free Pro year to students [3P, unverified] ([usagebar](https://usagebar.com/blog/kiro-pricing-and-rate-limits)).

## Conclusion

The economics of free AI coding flipped in 2026. In 2025, vendors bought adoption with generous free agents: Gemini CLI's 1,000 requests a day, Qwen's OAuth quota, Cursor's student year. By late 2026, each of those had been withdrawn or metered into credits. Free capacity now comes mostly from interchangeable model sources plugged into agents you own. The durable asset is therefore the open-source agent layer and your familiarity with it. Any vendor's free tier is a temporary subsidy that can be cut, as Google showed in June.

The practical result is that the gap between "free" and "Claude Code" is now mostly a model-quality gap, not a tooling gap. OpenCode, Cline and Kilo reproduce most of the Claude Code workflow at no cost. What money buys is sustained access to a frontier model. For many developers the smartest spend is not a $20 subscription but a $10 OpenRouter top-up or a few dollars of DeepSeek tokens behind a free agent. Moving up to Claude Pro or ChatGPT Plus makes sense once daily caps, not tool features, become the bottleneck. The biggest open questions are Antigravity's actual free quotas and the exact Codex Free limits. Both are unpublished, and either could change this ranking if Google or OpenAI chose to compete on free agentic capacity again.

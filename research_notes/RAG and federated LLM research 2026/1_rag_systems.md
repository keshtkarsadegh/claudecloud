## Q1. What are the main recent research directions in RAG (2024–2026)?

### Takeaway
Since 2025, the field has moved from fixed "retrieve-then-read" pipelines to **(a) RL-trained agentic search** (Search-R1 and similar), **(b) structured, graph or memory-based retrieval** (GraphRAG, LightRAG, HippoRAG 2), **(c) vision-native multimodal retrieval** (ColPali/ColQwen, VisRAG), and **(d) reasoning-intensive retrieval benchmarks** (BRIGHT). Careful 2025 benchmarks show that **neither long context nor RAG wins outright** (LaRA, ICML 2025). Graph-based RAG beats vanilla RAG mainly on multi-hop or complex reasoning and can cost far more tokens. Security, privacy and federated RAG became a distinct sub-field in 2025–2026.

### Cited Findings

**Survey landscape (2025–2026)**
- *A Systematic Literature Review of RAG: Techniques, Metrics, and Challenges* (Brown, Roman, Devereux; arXiv 2508.06401, Aug 2025, v3 Sept 2025). It screens 128 highly cited articles from 2020 to May 2025, with a lower citation threshold for 2025 papers. Its data stops in May 2025. — [arXiv 2508.06401](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.06401); [v3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.06401v3)
- *Knowledge-oriented RAG survey* (Cheng et al.; arXiv 2503.10677, Mar 2025). It covers retrieval mechanisms, generation, and how the two are integrated. — [arXiv 2503.10677](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.10677)
- *RAG: A Comprehensive Survey of Architectures, Enhancements, and Robustness Frontiers* (C. Sharma; arXiv 2506.00054, ~May 2025). Its taxonomy is retriever-centric / generator-centric / hybrid / robustness-oriented. It names adaptive retrieval, real-time retrieval integration, multi-hop reasoning and privacy-preserving retrieval as open challenges, along with the trade-offs of precision vs. flexibility and efficiency vs. faithfulness. — [arXiv 2506.00054](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.00054)
- *A Systematic Review of Key RAG Systems: Progress, Gaps, and Future Directions* (arXiv 2507.18910, Jul 2025). It compares retrieval accuracy, generation fluency, latency and compute efficiency across implementations. — [arXiv 2507.18910](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.18910)
- *Retrieval-Augmented Code Generation: A Survey with Focus on Repository-Level Approaches* (arXiv 2510.04905). It notes emerging hierarchical retrieval, memory compression and long-context reasoning for codebases. — [arXiv 2510.04905](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.04905v3)
- (Background) *RAG for AI-Generated Content: A Survey* (arXiv 2402.19473, 2024). — [arXiv 2402.19473](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.19473)

**1. Advanced retrieval: hybrid, rerankers, late interaction**
- *ColBERT late interaction (background).* Query and document are encoded separately with BERT, then scored by a lightweight token-level "MaxSim" interaction. Document representations can be precomputed offline, which keeps query-time cost low. — [Jina AI explainer](https://jina.ai/news/what-is-colbert-and-late-interaction-and-why-they-matter-in-search)
- *Jina-ColBERT-v2* (Jina AI, released 31 Aug 2024). A multilingual late-interaction model covering 89 languages, with flexible (Matryoshka-style) output dimensions and up to 8192-token context. — [Jina AI](https://jina.ai/news/what-is-colbert-and-late-interaction-and-why-they-matter-in-search); [AWS Marketplace listing](https://aws.amazon.com/marketplace/pp/prodview-yv2fnufantdl4)
- *BRIGHT: A Realistic and Challenging Benchmark for Reasoning-Intensive Retrieval* (Su et al.; Princeton et al.; ICLR 2025; arXiv 2407.12883). Its queries need reasoning, not lexical or semantic matching, and span economics, psychology, mathematics and coding. The best MTEB model scored only **18.0 nDCG@10**. Adding LLM-generated reasoning to queries raised retrieval by up to **12.2 points**, and better retrieval raised downstream QA by **6.6+ points**. Sources disagree on the query count (1,398 vs. 1,384) and on the best score (24.3 vs. 22.1). — [arXiv 2407.12883](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.12883); [ICLR 2025 proceedings](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/7a0f8055c838df8e62329a76c7c6403d-Abstract-Conference.html); [mlanthology](https://mlanthology.org/iclr/2025/su2025iclr-bright/)

**2. GraphRAG / knowledge-graph RAG**
- *HippoRAG 2 — "From RAG to Memory: Non-Parametric Continual Learning for LLMs"* (Jiménez Gutiérrez, Shu, Qi, Zhou, Yu Su; OSU NLP; **ICML 2025**, PMLR v267 pp. 21497–21515; arXiv 2502.14802). It builds on Personalized PageRank over an LLM-built KG and adds deeper passage integration and online LLM filtering. It outperforms standard RAG on factual, sense-making and associative memory tasks, with a **+7% associative-task gain over a SOTA embedding model** (a third-party summary reports "+7.7" against a different baseline). It is framed as non-parametric continual learning. Code: github.com/OSU-NLP-Group/HippoRAG. — [arXiv 2502.14802](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.14802); [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/gutierrez25a.html); [ICML poster](https://icml.cc/virtual/2025/poster/45585); [lunadong summary](https://papers.lunadong.com/paper/4542)
- *When to use Graphs in RAG: A Comprehensive Analysis for Graph RAG* (**GraphRAG-Bench**, HK PolyU; arXiv 2506.05690). On a leaderboard built from a novel corpus, HippoRAG2 ranks first (56.48), with LightRAG and MS-GraphRAG lower. A secondary summary reports Complex-Reasoning accuracy of 53.38 for HippoRAG2 vs. 42.93 for a comparison system. It also reports average prompt tokens of **vanilla RAG 879, HippoRAG2 1,008, MS-GraphRAG local 38,707, LightRAG 100,832, and global mode 331,375**, so the cost gap is two to three orders of magnitude. (These numbers come from secondary or aggregator pages.) — [arXiv 2506.05690](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.05690); [alphaXiv leaderboard](https://www.alphaxiv.org/benchmarks/the-hong-kong-polytechnic-university/graphrag-bench); [Substack summary](https://aiexpjourney.substack.com/p/do-you-really-need-graphrag-ai-innovations)
- *WildGraphBench* average accuracy (aggregator page): HippoRAG2 64.33%, NaiveRAG 59.79%, **LightRAG 56.76% (below naive RAG)**. MS-GraphRAG (global) was best on multi-fact accuracy at 47.64%. — [benchmarklist.com](https://benchmarklist.com/benchmarks/wildgraphbench/)
- *Legal-domain QA comparison* (CEUR-WS Vol-4079, RAGE-KG 2025). It compares HippoRAG 2, nano-GraphRAG, LightRAG and LlamaIndex with naive RAG. **Hybrid modes (LightRAG "mix", LlamaIndex hybrid) did best; KG-only systems did worse.** — [CEUR-WS paper](https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-4079/paper6.pdf)
- *RAGU: A Multi-Step GraphRAG Engine with a Compact Domain-Adapted LLM* (arXiv 2607.11683, 2026). It shows that verbose outputs can inflate GraphRAG scores. Under controlled formatting RAGU ties or slightly beats HippoRAG 2 on BioASQ (72.9 vs. 72.4), and HippoRAG 2 keeps a clear lead only on MuSiQue. — [arXiv 2607.11683](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.11683)
- 2026 domain and multimodal GraphRAG variants: *LEGO* (expert GraphRAG + expert CoT for legal reasoning; arXiv 2609.27009) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.27009); *HVM-GraphRAG* (holistic-view multimodal graph RAG on complex documents; arXiv 2607.24861) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.24861)

**3. Agentic RAG and iterative / multi-hop retrieval**
- *Agentic RAG: A Survey on Agentic RAG* (Singh, Ehtesham, Kumar, Talaei Khoei; arXiv 2501.09136, Jan 2025, v4 Apr 2026). Its taxonomy is organized by agent cardinality, control structure and autonomy (single-agent, multi-agent, graph-based). Its open issues are multi-agent coordination, process-aware evaluation, memory, efficiency and governance. It argues that output-only metrics are insufficient and that benchmarks should score reasoning trajectories and cost. The authors' GitHub repo is linked from the paper. — [arXiv 2501.09136v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.09136v4); [v3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.09136v3)
- ACL Anthology also lists a survey of *reasoning agentic RAG* (System 1 vs. System 2; Jintao Liang et al.) and a *data-centric survey of agentic RAG* (2026 entry). I did not verify their arXiv IDs. — [ACL Anthology (Su Gang)](https://aclanthology.org/people/sugang/); [ACL Anthology (Jihao Huang)](https://aclanthology.org/people/jihao-huang/)
- *Chain of Evidence: Pixel-Level Visual Attribution for Iterative RAG* (arXiv 2605.01284, 2026) applies iterative RAG with visual grounding. — [arXiv 2605.01284](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.01284)

**4. RAG training: RL for search (the 2025 "R1-style" wave)**
- *Search-R1: Training LLMs to Reason and Leverage Search Engines with RL* (Bowen Jin et al. (UIUC-led); **COLM 2025**; arXiv 2503.09516, v1 Mar 2025, v5 Aug 2025). The model interleaves multi-turn search calls with reasoning during RL rollouts. It uses **retrieved-token masking** for stable RL and a **simple outcome-based reward**. Across 7 QA datasets it reports gains of +41% (Qwen2.5-7B) and +20% (3B) over RAG baselines in v3; a later revision reports smaller gains (~26%/21% per the search summary), so the numbers differ across versions. It is built on veRL. Code: github.com/PeterGriffinJin/Search-R1. — [arXiv 2503.09516v5](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.09516v5); [v3 abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.09516v3); [VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/ai/beyond-rag-search-r1-integrates-search-engines-directly-into-reasoning-models/); [SourcePulse repo listing](https://www.sourcepulse.org/projects/1842941)
- *R1-Searcher: Incentivizing the Search Capability in LLMs via RL* (Huatong Song et al. (RUC); arXiv 2503.05592, Mar 2025). Two-stage outcome-based RL lets the model learn when and how to call retrieval. It reports significantly beating prior RAG methods and even GPT-4o-mini on four multi-hop QA datasets. — [arXiv 2503.05592](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.05592); [HF papers](https://huggingface.co/papers/2503.05592)
- *ZeroSearch: Incentivize the Search Capability of LLMs without Searching* (Hao Sun et al.; arXiv 2505.04588, May 2025). A simulated LLM "search engine" (≥3B) replaces live APIs, with a **curriculum that gradually degrades the quality of generated documents**, removing API cost. It groups Search-R1 and R1-Searcher (local corpus) apart from DeepResearcher and WebThinker (live commercial search). — [arXiv 2505.04588](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.04588)
- *ReSearch* (arXiv 2503.25094, as given in search snippets). RL that treats search operations as part of the reasoning chain, without supervision on intermediate steps. **Attribution from snippet, unverified.** — [Emergent Mind topic page](https://www.emergentmind.com/topics/research)
- *DeepResearcher*: RL against real web search engines (per ZeroSearch's related work). I did not retrieve its own paper. — [ZeroSearch arXiv 2505.04588](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.04588)

**5. Long context vs. RAG**
- *LaRA: Benchmarking RAG and Long-Context LLMs — No Silver Bullet for LC or RAG Routing* (Kuan Li et al.; **ICML 2025**, PMLR v267 pp. 36846–36867; arXiv 2502.09977). It has 2,326 test cases across 4 QA task types over novels, academic papers and financial statements, and evaluates 7 open and 4 proprietary LLMs. The best choice depends on model size, long-context ability, context length, task type and chunk characteristics. **LC does better on reasoning and comparison tasks.** A third-party summary reports RAG beating LC by 38.12% on Mistral-Nemo-12B at 128k while LC wins on GPT-4o; I could not verify this against the paper. — [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/li25dv.html); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.09977v2); [ICML poster](https://icml.cc/virtual/2025/poster/46069); [papers.cool](https://papers.cool/arxiv/2502.09977); [lunadong summary](https://papers.lunadong.com/paper/4999)

**6. Multimodal / visual-document RAG**
- *ColPali: Efficient Document Retrieval with Vision Language Models* (Faysse et al.; Illuin Tech / CentraleSupélec et al.; **ICLR 2025**; arXiv 2407.01449). PaliGemma-based multi-vector page-image embeddings with late-interaction scoring, needing no OCR or layout pipeline. It argues the bottleneck is the ingestion pipeline, not the embedder. It "largely outperforms" modern document-retrieval pipelines while being much faster and end-to-end trainable, and it introduces the **ViDoRe** benchmark. Code: github.com/illuin-tech/colpali. — [arXiv 2407.01449](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.01449v1); [ICLR 2025 poster](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2025/poster/28336); [mlanthology](https://mlanthology.org/iclr/2025/faysse2025iclr-colpali); [ViDoRe readme](https://vidore-readme.static.hf.space)
- *ColQwen2* swaps PaliGemma for Qwen2-VL as the backbone to get better retrieval. It is commonly deployed with Vespa. — [Analytics Vidhya](https://www.analyticsvidhya.com/blog/2024/10/multimodal-retrieval-with-colqwen-vespa/); [Decoding ML](https://decodingml.substack.com/p/the-king-of-multi-modal-rag-colpali)
- *VisRAG* (arXiv 2410.10594). According to a secondary source, it pools each page into a single VLM vector (a bi-encoder) instead of patch-level MaxSim, which gives faster indexing and smaller storage but weaker recall. **Primary source not verified.** — [traeai learning page](https://learn.traeai.com/t/ai-engineering/phases/12-multimodal-ai/23-colpali-vision-native-rag.html)

**7. Evaluation**
- *RAGChecker: A Fine-grained Framework for Diagnosing RAG* (Dongyu Ru, Lin Qiu et al.; Amazon; **NeurIPS 2024 Datasets & Benchmarks**; arXiv 2408.08067; DOI 10.52202/079017-0692). Claim-level diagnostic metrics cover both the retriever (claim recall, context precision) and the generator (faithfulness, noise sensitivity, hallucination, context utilization). It correlates with human judgment significantly better than other metrics and was applied to 8 RAG systems. Code: github.com/amazon-science/RAGChecker. It positions RAGAS, TruLens and ARES as offering fine-grained evaluation only on some metrics. — [NeurIPS proceedings](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/27245589131d17368cccdfa990cbf16e-Abstract.html); [arXiv 2408.08067](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.08067v2); [Amazon Science](https://www.amazon.science/publications/ragchecker-a-fine-grained-framework-for-diagnosing-retrieval-augmented-generation)
- BRIGHT (retrieval-side reasoning benchmark), LaRA (LC vs. RAG), GraphRAG-Bench and WildGraphBench (graph RAG) are covered above.
- Independent framework benchmark (Oct 2026): see Q2.

**8. Security, privacy and federated RAG**
- *Federated RAG: A Systematic Mapping Study* (Chakraborty, Dahal, Vivek Gupta; **Findings of EMNLP 2025**; arXiv 2505.18906). About 50 candidates (2020–2025) were narrowed to **18 primary studies**, classified by architecture, contribution type and domain. Confidentiality techniques include secure enclaves (TEE), homomorphic encryption and differential privacy. Its open problems are privacy-preserving retrieval, cross-client heterogeneity and evaluation, and it calls for shared benchmarks and stronger threat models. Example: **C-FedRAG** for clinical QA, where records stay in the hospital. — [arXiv 2505.18906](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.18906); [ACL Anthology preview](https://preview.aclanthology.org/dashboard/2025.findings-emnlp.388); [papers.cool](https://papers.cool/venue/2025.findings-emnlp.388@ACL)
- *Riddle Me This! Stealthy Membership Inference for RAG* (Interrogation Attack; Ashish Hooda et al.; **CCS 2025**; arXiv 2502.00306). A black-box membership inference attack (MIA) uses natural-language questions that only the target document can answer. It succeeds with **~30 queries** while staying stealthy and gets about 2× TPR@1%FPR over prior attacks. Prior RAG MIAs are easy to detect. — [arXiv 2502.00306](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.00306); [HF papers](https://huggingface.co/papers/2502.00306)
- *Is My Data in Your Retrieval Database? MIAs Against RAG* (Anderson et al., IBM Research; arXiv 2405.20446, rev. Feb 2025). A black-box or gray-box MIA via crafted prompts, plus a prompt-template defense. — [arXiv 2405.20446](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.20446v2)
- *Securing RAG: A Taxonomy of Attacks, Defenses, and Future Directions* (arXiv 2604.08304, 2026). It organizes threats around the knowledge-access pipeline (knowledge corruption, retrieval-time access manipulation, context exploitation, exfiltration) and argues for layered, boundary-aware defenses. — [arXiv 2604.08304](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.08304v1)
- *Security and Privacy in RAG: Architectures, Threats, Defenses, and Future Directions* (arXiv 2606.25533, 2026). It catalogs membership inference, index inference and poisoning attacks and lists open challenges for trustworthy RAG. — [arXiv 2606.25533](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.25533)

### Inferences
- **RL-for-search is the most active training direction in 2025–2026.** Within months Search-R1, R1-Searcher, ReSearch, ZeroSearch and DeepResearcher appeared, sharing outcome-only rewards, small Qwen2.5 backbones and multi-hop QA benchmarks (NQ/HotpotQA/2Wiki/MuSiQue). The split between local-corpus RL, simulated-search RL (ZeroSearch) and live-web RL (DeepResearcher) is a useful axis for a taxonomy.
- **GraphRAG's value depends on the task.** Several independent benchmarks (GraphRAG-Bench, WildGraphBench, legal QA, RAGU) agree that graph methods help on multi-hop or global questions but can match or underperform naive or hybrid RAG on simple fact lookup. Some graph methods (LightRAG, MS-GraphRAG global) cost 40–400× more prompt tokens than vanilla RAG. HippoRAG 2 is the notable exception: it is near vanilla-RAG token cost while leading accuracy.
- **Evaluation format matters.** RAGU's finding that verbosity inflates LLM-judge scores suggests that many reported GraphRAG gains may be partly artifacts of how answers are formatted.
- **Long context vs. RAG is now framed as routing.** LaRA's "no silver bullet" result points toward adaptive routing (deciding per query whether to retrieve or put everything in context) rather than replacement.
- **Visual-document RAG is converging on late interaction over page images.** The ColPali/ColQwen line skips OCR. The open trade-off is storage for multi-vector indexes vs. single-vector recall (the VisRAG style).
- **Privacy work is maturing from attacks toward systems.** Membership inference now works with about 30 stealthy queries, which makes "keep data in the retrieval index rather than in the weights" insufficient as a privacy argument. Federated RAG is still small (18 primary studies as of 2025) and lacks benchmarks.

## Q2. What are the leading open-source RAG frameworks in 2026 and how do they differ?

### Takeaway
By 2026 the frameworks have settled into distinct roles. **LlamaIndex** is strongest for data ingestion and indexing. **LangChain + LangGraph** handles durable, stateful agentic or multi-step retrieval. **Haystack** offers explicit, testable production pipelines with built-in evaluators. **RAGFlow** is a turnkey self-hosted engine with deep PDF and document parsing. **DSPy** is a metric-driven optimizer or compiler for prompts and retrieval modules rather than a classic RAG framework. **GraphRAG, LightRAG and HippoRAG** are specialized graph-retrieval libraries. On a simple retrieve-and-answer task, answer quality across the general frameworks is a statistical tie.

### Cited Findings
- **Independent benchmark (Oct 2026):** the same retrieve-and-answer pipeline was run in LangChain, LlamaIndex, Haystack, DSPy and plain Python on one scraped 310-page docs corpus, plus LightRAG and GraphRAG. Answer correctness was **0.641 (LangChain, Haystack, DSPy) vs. 0.631 (LlamaIndex)**, a statistical tie. The only real difference was **context precision, where Haystack led**. It is a single run on a single corpus. — [GitHub: yourtechmafia/rag-frameworks-benchmark](https://github.com/yourtechmafia/rag-frameworks-benchmark)
- **Positioning:** Haystack gives explicit pipelines with built-in evaluators. LangChain + LangGraph adds durable agentic retrieval. LlamaIndex has the widest data integrations. RAGFlow is a ready self-hosted RAG engine with strong document parsing. — [Firecrawl: 15 Best Open-Source RAG Frameworks in 2026](https://www.firecrawl.dev/blog/best-open-source-rag-frameworks); [techsy.io](https://techsy.io/en/blog/best-rag-framework-2026); [saaro blog](https://blog.saaro.net/en/rag-frameworks-2026-llamaindex-langchain-langgraph-haystack-)
- **DSPy** is "not a RAG framework in the classic sense, but a programmatic optimizer that compiles prompts and retrieval modules from data". It suits teams that want to optimize against a metric. — [ScrapingBee: Best RAG Frameworks 2026](https://www.scrapingbee.com/blog/best-rag-frameworks/); [vdf.ai](https://vdf.ai/blog/best-rag-frameworks/)
- **Selection heuristics:** LlamaIndex for document-heavy ingestion, LangGraph for agentic multi-step retrieval, Haystack for testable production pipelines, RAGFlow for turnkey self-hosting with deep PDF parsing, DSPy for metric-driven optimization. — [devtoollab: Frameworks vs Managed Services](https://devtoollab.com/blog/best-rag-platforms-and-tools); [Firecrawl](https://www.firecrawl.dev/blog/best-open-source-rag-frameworks)
- **Versions and licenses as reported by 2026 blogs (secondary, verify against release notes):** LangChain 1.4.3, LangGraph 1.2.13, Haystack 3.3.0, RAGFlow 1.0.0-rc1, DSPy 3.4.0. MIT: LlamaIndex, LangChain, LangGraph, DSPy, LightRAG. Apache-2.0: Haystack, RAGFlow, txtai, Kotaemon. — [techsy.io](https://techsy.io/en/blog/best-rag-framework-2026); [Firecrawl](https://www.firecrawl.dev/blog/best-open-source-rag-frameworks)
- **Graph-RAG libraries:** HippoRAG 2 (github.com/OSU-NLP-Group/HippoRAG). Benchmarks comparing it with LightRAG and MS-GraphRAG are in Q1. — [arXiv 2502.14802](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.14802); [GraphRAG-Bench](https://www.alphaxiv.org/benchmarks/the-hong-kong-polytechnic-university/graphrag-bench)
- **RL-search training stacks:** Search-R1 (veRL-based) is the reference open-source codebase for RL-trained search agents. — [SourcePulse](https://www.sourcepulse.org/projects/1842941); [arXiv 2503.09516](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.09516v5)
- **Multimodal retrieval library:** illuin-tech/colpali (ColPali/ColQwen training and inference). — [Algolia DocSearch index of repo](https://docsearch.algolia.com/mcp/docs/repo/illuin-tech/colpali); [ViDoRe readme](https://vidore-readme.static.hf.space)
- **Evaluation tooling:** RAGChecker (amazon-science/RAGChecker). RAGAS, TruLens and ARES are cited as partial-coverage alternatives. — [arXiv 2408.08067](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.08067v2)

### Inferences
- Because answer quality is nearly identical on a basic pipeline, the choice between frameworks depends on **operational concerns**: ingestion breadth, agent state and durability, testability and evaluation hooks, self-hosting, and optimization. A researcher should pick based on the experimental variable being studied (for example, DSPy for prompt or program optimization, LangGraph for agent control-flow research, and FlashRAG-style toolkits for reproducible academic baselines, which are unverified here).
- Specialized graph-RAG libraries should be chosen on task type and token budget, given the 40–400× prompt-token spread in GraphRAG-Bench.

## Q3. What are the open problems and promising research gaps?

### Takeaway
Recurring open problems are: **(1)** evaluating *process* (trajectories, cost) and not only final answers for agentic RAG; **(2)** knowing *when* structured or graph retrieval pays for its cost; **(3)** adaptive routing between long context and RAG; **(4)** reasoning-intensive retrieval, where SOTA retrievers score below about 25 nDCG@10 on BRIGHT; **(5)** security, poisoning and privacy, with stealthy membership inference and a lack of layered defenses; **(6)** federated or private RAG, which lacks benchmarks, threat models and ways to handle heterogeneity; **(7)** efficiency and governance of multi-agent RAG; and **(8)** robustness and faithfulness trade-offs.

### Cited Findings
- **Agentic RAG:** open issues are multi-agent coordination, process-aware evaluation, memory management, computational efficiency and governance. Output-only metrics are insufficient, and benchmarks should score reasoning trajectories and cost. — [arXiv 2501.09136v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.09136v4)
- **General RAG:** adaptive retrieval architectures, real-time retrieval integration, multi-hop reasoning over evidence, and privacy-preserving retrieval, plus the trade-offs of retrieval precision vs. generation flexibility and efficiency vs. faithfulness. — [arXiv 2506.00054](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.00054)
- **Reasoning-intensive retrieval:** the best MTEB embedder reaches only 18.0 nDCG@10 on BRIGHT. LLM query reasoning adds up to +12.2, which shows that retrievers lack reasoning. — [arXiv 2407.12883](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.12883)
- **Long context vs. RAG:** no single winner; the choice depends on model, context length, task and chunking. This is an open routing problem. — [PMLR v267 LaRA](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/li25dv.html)
- **Graph RAG cost and accuracy:** graph methods can multiply prompt tokens (LightRAG ~100k vs. vanilla ~0.9k per query), and some underperform naive RAG on general benchmarks. — [GraphRAG-Bench summary](https://aiexpjourney.substack.com/p/do-you-really-need-graphrag-ai-innovations); [WildGraphBench](https://benchmarklist.com/benchmarks/wildgraphbench/). Answer verbosity confounds LLM-judged comparisons. — [RAGU arXiv 2607.11683](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.11683)
- **RAG as memory and continual learning:** HippoRAG 2 frames RAG as non-parametric continual learning but notes vector retrieval does not yet capture human-like associative memory. — [arXiv 2502.14802](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.14802)
- **RL-for-search cost:** live-search RL is expensive and noisy because of API cost and uncontrolled document quality. That motivated ZeroSearch's simulated-retrieval curriculum, which suggests training environments for search agents are an open problem. — [arXiv 2505.04588](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.04588)
- **Security:** threats span knowledge corruption, retrieval-time access manipulation, context exploitation and exfiltration, and the paper calls for layered, boundary-aware defenses. — [arXiv 2604.08304](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.08304v1). Membership inference, index inference and poisoning remain open. — [arXiv 2606.25533](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.25533). Stealthy MIA works with about 30 queries. — [arXiv 2502.00306](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.00306)
- **Federated RAG:** gaps in privacy-preserving retrieval, cross-client heterogeneity and evaluation, with a need for shared benchmarks and stronger threat models. Only 18 primary studies exist (to 2025). — [arXiv 2505.18906](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.18906)
- **Evaluation:** existing frameworks (RAGAS, TruLens, ARES) cover only some fine-grained metrics. RAGChecker's claim-level diagnostics improve human correlation, but they still depend on an LLM judge. — [arXiv 2408.08067](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.08067v2)

### Inferences (promising PhD-level research gaps)
- **Retrievers trained with RL alongside reasoners.** Current RL-search work trains the *policy* (the generator) and keeps the retriever fixed. Co-training retriever and reasoner, or adding reasoning-aware rerankers, could address BRIGHT-style failures.
- **Process rewards for search agents.** Search-R1 and R1-Searcher use outcome-only rewards. Combining them with step-level evidence-quality or attribution rewards, which ties into the survey's call for trajectory-level evaluation, is under-explored.
- **Cost-aware adaptive routing.** A principled router that chooses among no retrieval, vanilla RAG, graph RAG, and long context per query, with explicit token and latency budgets, is suggested jointly by LaRA and GraphRAG-Bench's cost spread.
- **Fair GraphRAG evaluation.** Benchmarks should control for answer formatting and verbosity, report token cost, and separate local from global questions.
- **Federated and private RAG** with formal guarantees (DP retrieval, TEE-backed indexes) that are evaluated against the newer stealthy MIAs (Interrogation Attack). Cross-silo heterogeneity, where each client has a different embedder or corpus distribution, is an open area with very few primary studies. This is directly relevant to the "federated LLM" theme of the wider report.
- **Defenses against poisoning in agentic loops.** Agents that issue many queries widen the attack surface, and boundary-aware defenses are still conceptual.
- **Multimodal efficiency.** Multi-vector page embeddings (ColPali) have high storage cost. Compression, token pooling and hybrid single-plus-multi-vector indexes remain open (based on the ColPali vs. VisRAG trade-off from secondary sources).

### Gaps

- No verified primary data was obtained on hallucination rates under RAG vs. no-RAG for frontier 2026 models.
